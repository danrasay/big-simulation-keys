**MGT155 ****Course Project: “The Big Simulation”**

**Instructor Manual**

**Summary note**

Because this project is designed to facilitate student learning of the real-life financial reporting, the majority of the questions are open-ended. There is no one single correct answer most questions. That being said, the financial ratio calculations have fixed solutions, and the investigator’s report should more or less fall within the proposed solutions (see end of this manual).

While the project allows flexible answers, the bases upon which the instructor grades the students are explicitly laid out in the “*Grading will be based on*” section for each role. For example, in role I (“disruptor”), the grading is based on:

*“**The **ability to answer reasonable questions with your system design; thoughtfulness and comprehensiveness of your system in meeting the above requirements; understandability compared to current accounting system; susceptibility to possible fraud and manipulations; and quality of delivery/presentation in persuading the audience.**”*

The instructor can also choose to grade all roles based on the quality of presentation or the provision of certain results. For example, a Spring 2022 project was graded based on:

- Quality of oral presentation and write report (100 points)
- Completion of the required tasks (100 points)

Whichever way the instructor decides to use this project, the goal is to facilitate student learning through as hands-on an approach as possible. Good luck!

**Solutions to Role I****V**** (“Investigator”)**** **

Material changes from the previous year & possible explanations:

1. Cash and cash equivalent in 2022 is $1000MM higher than cash in 2021, while net receivables are higher in a similar amount. The company may have factored their accounts receivable, resulting in an increase in their cash ratio and cash flows.

2.   Merchandise inventories almost doubles that of the previous year, indicating potential change in inventory method, however, relevant footnote is missing regarding this change.

3. Gross property and equipment is higher while depreciation remains about the same, suggesting that there might be missing depreciation expenses or a change in depreciation method that is missing in the footnotes.

4. Goodwill is 20% higher than the previous year. Since under GAAP there is no reversing the impairment loss on goodwill, it is likely that the company has made new acquisitions. If so, the footnote should reflect this.

5. Accounts payable is almost 30% lower than the previous year, the company may be doing better with their payment policy. This possibility is worth confirming in the related footnotes.

6. Revenue is up $6000MM while COGS is down over $7000MM, it is possible that the company has changed their inventory method from LIFO to FIFO. If so, this should be reflected in the footnotes.

Other answers are acceptable as long as they are supported by relevant evidence in the financial statements.
